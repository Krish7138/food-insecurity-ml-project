Limitations: Why the Food Model Accuracy Did Not Improve
    - The available data may not include all important factors related to food insecurity.
    - Food insecurity is a complex issue and can change quickly over time.
    - Some survey answers may be incomplete, inaccurate, or self-reported with bias.
    - Important variables such as debt, housing costs, savings, and local food prices may be missing.
    - There may be overlap between food secure and food insecure groups, making prediction harder.
    - If one class has more cases than the other, the model may struggle to learn both groups well.
    - Many categorical survey variables can create noisy or weak signals.
    - Different machine learning models gave similar results, suggesting the main limitation is the data rather than the algorithm.


Future Steps to Improve Performance
    - Collect more data with a larger sample size.
    - Add stronger variables such as debt, rent burden, benefits, and household expenses.
    - Use a better measure of food insecurity based on multiple questions instead of one variable.
    - Clean missing values and improve data quality.
    - Balance the classes using sampling methods or class weights.
    - Combine rare categories to reduce noise.
    - Try feature selection to keep only the most useful variables.
    - Build separate models for different groups (families, older adults, single parents).
    - Use additional evaluation metrics such as recall, precision, and F1-score, not only accuracy.
    - Test newer data to improve prediction of recent food insecurity trends.
